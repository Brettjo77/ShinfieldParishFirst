<h1>What a parish councillor does</h1>

Shinfield Parish Council is the most local level of government. Its councillors decide how a budget of about {{ site.figures.precept }} a year is spent, look after local facilities, and speak for residents to Wokingham Borough Council.

The parish part of council tax for a Band D home is {{ site.figures.bandD }} in {{ site.figures.bandDYear }}. Councillors are unpaid volunteers.

<p class="small-note">Figures from Shinfield Parish Council and Wokingham Borough Council, last checked {{ site.figures.lastChecked | longDate }}.</p>

<h2>What the parish council can and can't do</h2>
<div class="two-col">
<div class="panel panel-can">
<h3>The parish council can</h3>
<ul>
<li>Set the parish precept and decide how it is spent</li>
<li>Run and improve sports pitches, play areas, open spaces and community halls</li>
<li>Make grants to local clubs and groups</li>
<li>Comment on every planning application in the parish</li>
<li>Shape planning through the Neighbourhood Plan</li>
<li>Use its share of developer money (CIL) on local projects</li>
<li>Act for the wellbeing of the parish under the General Power of Competence, where it qualifies</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="panel panel-cannot">
<h3>The parish council can't</h3>
<ul>
<li>Decide planning applications. Wokingham Borough Council does, including Loddon Garden Village</li>
<li>Fix roads, potholes or traffic. That's the borough</li>
<li>Run schools, bin collections or social care</li>
<li>Set the main council tax, only the parish part</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

## What the job involves

- A full council meeting about once a month, in the evening, open to the public
- One or two committees, such as Facilities or Planning
- Reading papers, visiting sites and talking to residents
- Around {{ site.timeCommitment }} in total
- Training is offered, and the Clerk is a qualified professional who advises the council

## Why it matters in 2027

The council grows to {{ site.seats }} seats in May 2027, so {{ site.majority }} councillors make a majority. Whoever holds those seats sets the parish's priorities for four years.
